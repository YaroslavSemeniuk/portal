Design Estimate

Echo Recap

A Spotify recap app that turns listening history into a personal echo — mood-based colours, a journey across day, week, month, season and year, and cards built to be shared. A working prototype already exists; this engagement is a redesign of it, not a first draft, so the starting point is a product whose structure can be examined rather than imagined.

The visual direction is already set by the client: dark theme, neon accents, minimalism, with Spotify as the reference point. That removes the exploratory phase most redesigns pay for, and it is why the moodboard sits inside the concept block as a short selection step rather than a stage of its own. The timeline is tied to Spotify Wrapped season — the design has to be finished with enough runway for build.

Delivery plan Indicative allocation

Four blocks, with two approval gates inside the concept stage. The sequence matters: the audit finds what is structurally broken, the moodboard settles what the product should look like, and only then does the concept commit that look to real screens. Each gate closes one open question before the next stage can spend hours on it — which on a fixed-price engagement is what protects the budget. A direction change caught at hour 20 is a conversation; the same change caught at hour 110 is a rebuild.

Audit 18 h€700

The current prototype is broken down into a flow diagram and read for bottlenecks. Several are already visible: the profile screen is populated with data that onboarding never asks for, there is no error state anywhere, and there is no forgot-password path. These are structural, not cosmetic — redrawing the screens without resolving them would ship the same problems in a better-looking form.

  • Flow diagram of the current app — every screen and transition as it behaves today
  • Prioritised UX issue list — what breaks the user, what is merely untidy, and which of the two this engagement fixes
  • Data-source check — for each field on screen, where the value comes from and whether anything actually collects it
  • Review round included — two of the eighteen hours cover walking the findings with the client and correcting them
  • No competitor analysis — Stats.fm, Last.fm and Musicat are deliberately out. The direction is already set, so a survey would produce reading rather than decisions
Design concept 21.5 h€400

Moodboard and concept in one block, because they answer the same question at two levels of resolution. The moodboard proposes the look as references and options; the concept proves it on real screens. The client approves twice — once on the board, once on the screens — and the second approval is what releases the redesign.

  • Moodboard — references from Pinterest and shipped applications, plus pattern options: buttons, cards, footers, and how a neon accent behaves on a dark ground without vibrating
  • Colour range — the base palette, and the room reserved for mood-based colour on Recap
  • Moodboard approval — the client picks the options that carry forward. The board is the direction; nothing is restated as a separate document
  • Main Recap screen — the screen the product is judged on, drawn first, where mood-driven colour has to stay readable across every mood
  • One or two supporting screens — chosen at the moodboard gate for whatever the concept most needs to prove
  • Two revision rounds included — three of the hours here cover feedback on the board and on the screens
Redesign 89 h€900

The body of the engagement. Six flows, each drawn to the approved concept, including the states the prototype is missing today. The navigation model is the hard part throughout: the app moves across day, week, month, season and year, and every level has to make its own scroll feel like one deliberate step rather than an unbounded feed.

  • Recap / Echo — main screen — mood-based colour and journey navigation across day, week, month, season, year
  • Echo — detail — overview, tracks, artists, genres, and playlist export
  • Sessions — a timeline of listening sessions with drill-down into a single session
  • Year in Review / History — swipe navigation from year to season, one scroll per level
  • Sharing flow — Instagram Stories cards. The client names this as the main organic channel, so the cards are designed as a distribution surface, not an afterthought export
  • Empty and error states — "history not loaded", "no data yet", and the rest of what the prototype currently has no answer for
  • Consistency pass and Figma handoff — nine of the eighty-nine hours cover revision rounds, the pass that catches drift across six flows drawn over four weeks, and file preparation for development
Mascot 16 h€500

Carried as a fixed €500 allocation. Character work is a different discipline from interface design, and keeping it as its own line means this block can be adjusted or dropped without re-pricing anything else. Three things are delivered, and the third is a concept rather than production animation.

Decision on record

Agreed

How the mascot is delivered

Animation is a different discipline with a different cost base, and folding production animation into a fixed price would either inflate the whole engagement or quietly starve the flows. Rather than defer the question entirely, the mascot is delivered in a shape that answers it without paying for it.

Agreed: no Lottie. The €500 covers three things — component SVG, colour states, and an animation concept shown through short generated reference clips. The clips communicate intent; they are direction for whoever animates the mascot, not assets to ship.

Building the droplet as separate components — body, eyes, arms — is what makes this work later. An animator inherits a rigged character and a defined state set rather than a flat illustration to take apart first. Production animation stays a separate scope and a separate budget.

Assumptions

The fixed price holds while these remain true. Each is stated here so it can be confirmed or corrected before work begins rather than discovered at hour 90.

  • Scope is fixed at the concept gate The 144.5 hours cover the four blocks listed above. The audit produces the definitive screen list — if it comes out materially larger than the current prototype implies, the estimate is revisited at the gate rather than absorbed. Once the concept is approved the list is locked, and anything added after that is quoted separately.
  • The visual direction stays as briefed Dark theme, neon accents, minimalism, Spotify as reference. The moodboard is sized for choosing inside that direction, not for exploring a second one. A change of direction after Gate 2.2 is a new concept stage.
  • Mascot animation is concept only The €500 covers component SVG, colour states, and an animation concept illustrated with generated reference clips. Production animation — Lottie or finished video — is not included and is estimated on its own.
  • History import and device-level data are out of scope These are data and platform problems rather than design ones. No screens are drawn for them here.
  • No competitor analysis Stats.fm, Last.fm and Musicat are deliberately excluded. The direction is already decided, so the audit reads the product itself rather than the category.
  • Interface copy is supplied by the client Screen text, empty-state wording and share-card copy arrive as finished text. Copywriting is not in the 144.5 hours.
  • Feedback arrives within two business days per gate The October deadline depends on this more than on the hours. Slower turnaround moves the calendar without changing the scope, and the Wrapped window does not move with it.
