The page count and nesting depth aren't the problem on their own: the deepest URL on the site is only 4 segments long (e.g. /products/besa-research/features/connectivity/), which is fine for a 349-page catalog. The real issue is that the same information exists in two different branches of the tree at once — that's what's worth fixing in the new structure, not "how many levels deep it goes."

02 · Details

What we found — and why it matters right now

We grouped the findings by how much they affect decisions you need to make for the MVP soon — not just by "how bad the bug is."

Tier 1 — affects how the 5 MVP product pages should be built
Critical for MVP

No single product-page template

Where
/products/besa-research//products/besa-mri//products/besa-connectivity//products/besa-statistics//products/besa-simulator/
What it is
Each of the 6 current products has a slightly different set of sub-pages. Four of them have an "overview" page named <product>-overview, but BESA Connectivity's is just called overview, with no product name in it. Three products have a dedicated "features" index page; BESA Statistics has feature pages but no index page for them; BESA Connectivity has no features section at all.
Why it matters
You're designing right now how to present 5 products consistently. If the current structure is used as a reference "as is," this inconsistency carries straight into the new site.
What to do
Lock in one mandatory set of blocks for a product page (overview → capabilities → downloads → certificates if applicable) and apply it identically to all 5 — this is a page-design decision, not technical debt, so it's cheapest to fix now, before build.
Technical error

A redirect points to a different product's features

Where
/products/besa-statistics/features/
What it is
This address doesn't show an error — it silently redirects to /products/besa-research/features/, i.e. to a completely different product's capabilities.
Why it matters
Someone looking for what BESA Statistics can do ends up reading about BESA Research instead, and will most likely just leave without realizing the link was wrong.
What to do
These URLs will change anyway on the new site, so the redirect itself doesn't need fixing — what matters is not carrying this mix-up into the new site's redirect map. We're flagging it so whoever builds the redirects knows about it.
Needs your decision

Open question: which product is the "extra" one among the 6

Where
/products/eeg-focus/ (9 pages, live but unlinked)/home/downloads/besa-plot//contact/besa-plot-download/ (no product page of its own)
What it is
The current site effectively shows 6 product lines (BESA Research, MRI, Simulator, Connectivity, Statistics, EEG Focus), while the new site's MVP is 5. EEG Focus is already missing from the main menu and the Products page — nothing links to it, even though its pages are live and indexed. At the same time, BESA Plot has a download page and an order form, but no product page of its own at all.
Why it matters
This directly affects how many product templates need to be designed and built for the MVP.
What to do
We're not guessing on your behalf — flagging it as an open question below, in the "Proposed sitemap" section.
Matches your own idea

Downloads exist in two (in one case, three) parallel versions

Where
/products/<product>/downloads/ — for all 6 products/home/downloads/<product>/ — parallel copy, same 6 products/downloads2-besa_research/ — a third, older copy (BESA Research only)
What it is
The same "where to download" page exists at two different addresses (text overlap of 62–74%), and for BESA Research there's a third, clearly outdated copy (downloads2-besa_research).
Why it matters
When content gets updated in one place, the other copy goes stale — and the site starts contradicting itself.
What to do
This is exactly what you already mentioned on the call — that all downloads could live on one page. The audit confirms it: yes, worth doing, precisely because there are effectively three parallel versions right now.

04 · Looking ahead

What structure fits the new site

This draws not just on the audit of the current site, but on what you described on the call: an MVP for 5 products by December 1, priority on the homepage and the product world, and a segmentation that shouldn't force people into a rigid "are you a clinician or a researcher" split.

Open question 1

The final line-up of 5 MVP products. Your own discovery notes confirm two things: BESA Research is the flagship product, and BESA Statistics was flagged as either a standalone purchase or an add-on — which decides whether it needs a full product page or a lighter add-on treatment inside another product's page. The other slots aren't named yet. Based on current site navigation, EEG Focus looks like the most likely "extra" one (it's already missing from the menu) — but that's our inference, not your confirmed decision. BESA Plot's status also needs a call: a sixth product with its own page, or a feature/accessory folded into another product?

MVP — critical scope for December 1

/ — new homepage
/products/ — hub with 5 products (← pending open question 1)
every product: the same template — overview · capabilities (tagged EEG / MEG / both) · link to downloads
/downloads/ — one shared downloads hub with a product filter, instead of three parallel versions
/customers/ — page for existing customers (same entry point as /downloads/, or right next to it)
/contact/ — sales · distributor by region · contact form · career
/contact/report-incident/ — separate and visible, not buried among other forms
/contact/newsletter/ — subscribe + unsubscribe in one place (instead of three pages)
/about/ — brief company info (without the 21 team-member cards — not critical for launch)
Why the incident form gets its own line

You gave the example of a doctor needing to report an incident involving MEG equipment. That's not a marketing form — it carries operational and regulatory weight, so on the new site it shouldn't get lost among ordinary "contact us" links the way /contact/incident/ is currently lost among 11 other same-level contact pages.

Phase 2 — after the MVP launches

/about/team/ — team member cards (currently 21)
/science/ — publications and research
/events/ — conferences, webinars, workshops, merged into one section per your own suggestion
/news/ — the blog, once the team has sorted and cleaned it up
/faq/ — decide: a proper on-site section, or keep sending people to wiki.besa.de, but consistently for the index page and every child page

Format of the sitemap itself — a table for people, not a tree of links

A technical sitemap.xml (the one we analyzed) works for search engines and developers, but not for a manager making decisions. A flat list of pages tagged by "who it's for" and "which modality" is easier for you to work with — and it happens to solve your UX challenge too: not a rigid "clinician or researcher" fork, but tags that can be combined freely.

05 · Summary

Recommendations, point by point

Short, without repeating the detail above — what we suggest doing, and in what order.

  1. Close the open question on products.
    Agree on the final list of 5 MVP products — including the fate of EEG Focus and BESA Plot — before the team starts building product templates.
  2. One template for all 5 product pages.
    The same set of blocks (overview → capabilities tagged EEG/MEG → downloads), with no exceptions like the current BESA Connectivity.
  3. One downloads hub instead of three.
    Confirms the idea you already raised on the call — and the audit shows exactly why it's the right call: the current versions have already drifted apart.
  4. The incident-report form gets its own, visible spot.
    Don't bury it among ordinary contact links, given its regulatory weight.
  5. Merge the newsletter flow into one block.
    Right now it's three separate pages for one action.
  6. Don't carry technical debt over "by inertia."
    The test page, the broken ?page_id= links, the pipeline-eeg duplicate — none of these need a decision, they just shouldn't be migrated.
  7. Team, Publications, Events, Blog — deliberately Phase 2.
    This matches what you already defined as less critical for the December release.
  8. For any future structure discussion, use a table, not a menu tree.
    It's easier to see whether every audience and modality is covered, and it doesn't require technical knowledge to read.
06 · What happens next

Proposed first phase of work

Before jumping into visual design or rebuilding anything, we recommend starting the engagement with a short, structured phase. It's the fastest way to turn everything above — plus the material you're still finishing on your side — into a sitemap and a homepage concept you've actually signed off on, rather than one we assumed on your behalf.

  1. Bring in the full context

    Fold in the product/content material your team is currently preparing — the final product line-up (BESA Research confirmed as flagship, BESA Statistics flagged as standalone-or-add-on, the remaining slots still open), feature messaging, the exact list of forms, required languages, and the CMS decision — into one shared picture.

  2. Walk through the open questions together

    Go through the open items this audit surfaced (EEG Focus / BESA Plot, the incident and bug-report flows, distributor data, translation languages) and turn each into a documented decision instead of an assumption.

  3. Draft sitemap v1

    Build the first correct sitemap for the new site using this full, agreed context — not the current site's menu, and not our first-pass proposal above.

  4. Reference research

    Look at medtech and scientific-software companies that present their product offering well, and use them as design references — you mentioned it's hard to find a good example among direct competitors, so we broaden the search to the wider medtech and scientific-software space.

  5. Homepage & navigation concept

    Design a concept for the updated homepage and its navigation, grounded in sitemap v1 and the reference research — this is the piece that has to solve the clinician/researcher/EEG/MEG overlap you flagged as the core UX challenge.

  6. Align and sign off

    Review the concept with you (and IT, where relevant) before it becomes the basis for anything else — this is the checkpoint where direction gets locked in.

  7. Break down into logical blocks and milestones

    Only once the above is agreed: break the new site down into logical content blocks — not literal pages — move into block-by-block redesign, and sequence the build across milestones toward December 1.

We treat this as its own fixed-price phase, separate from general discovery — it's what lets us quote a firm price for each following phase and move forward stage by stage, with full visibility and control over both cost and scope at every step.
